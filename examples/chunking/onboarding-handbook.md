Welcome to the support team. Read this handbook in your first week; your lead will walk through it with you on day three.

# Your first week

**Day one.** You get access to the desk, the admin panel, and the team chat on your first morning. If any of the three does not work by noon, tell your lead, because the access request has to be approved by IT before the end of the day or it waits until the next weekly batch.

Spend the afternoon reading closed tickets from the last month. Pick tickets with the tags billing, login, and integrations, since those three tags make up most of the queue. Do not reply to any open tickets yet, even simple ones; customers see the name of whoever replies first, and the first reply sets their expectations for the whole conversation.

Write down every question you have while reading. Nothing is too small. Most questions new agents ask on day one are already answered in the knowledge base, and finding those answers is the fastest way to learn where things live.

At the end of the day, send your list of questions to your lead. They will mark the ones that are answered in the knowledge base so you can look them up, and answer the rest in your day two meeting.

**Day two.** On day two you shadow an experienced agent for the whole morning. Sit next to them, or join their screen share if you are remote. Watch how they read a ticket before replying: they check the customer's plan, the last three tickets from the same workspace, and any open incidents, before they type a single word.

Ask them why they chose each reply. Good agents often skip a step from the handbook on purpose, and the reason is usually more useful than the step itself. Note those reasons down; they become the edge cases you will meet later.

After lunch you draft replies to five open tickets, but you do not send them. Your shadow reviews each draft with you and either sends it as written, edits it, or explains why a different reply is better.

Finish the day by tagging twenty closed tickets that are missing tags. Tagging trains you to recognize ticket types quickly, which is what makes triage fast later.

**Day three.** Day three is your first day on the live queue, with a cap of ten tickets. Your lead reviews every reply you send that day within an hour of sending it.

Pick tickets from the top of the queue in order. Do not skip a ticket because it looks hard; flag it for your lead instead, and they will either take it or pair with you on it.

If a customer is angry, acknowledge the problem in the first sentence, say what you will do next, and give a time when they will hear back. Never promise a refund, a credit, or a fix date that is not already confirmed in the ticket or the knowledge base.

At the end of day three you and your lead review all ten tickets together. Expect most of the feedback to be about tone and about asking for missing information up front, rather than about product knowledge.

# Escalation

Escalate a ticket when it needs someone with more access, more authority, or more product knowledge than you have. Escalating early is always better than guessing.

Use the Escalate button, not a chat message. The button records who escalated, when, and why, and the ticket keeps its place in the queue's response time reporting.
